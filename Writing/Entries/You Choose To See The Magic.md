My dirty laundry has been staring me down with a defiant challenging stare. Next to this pile of procrastination I see my unpacked duffel bag from my recent trip. Both chores I have been avoiding for the last week. Ever since I got back from the lush lands of Iceland, there has been a sort of blue heaviness hanging over me. 

It is a familiar feeling, one that often visits me after a trip. Every time I remember a *something* from the trip I am also reminded of the passage of time, beautiful moments that are now gone. Only relived after I conjure the memory inside of me. 

As I sit in my sofa, staring out the window ignoring the challenge of my dirty clothes, I wonder how is it possible to grow such a strong attachment to a country where I have no roots. There's no nordic blood flowing/coursing through me, but there's a strong spiritual tie, a swelling desire to go back to what once was and relive the adventures I had with my family.  

Pushing the need for adventure aside and instead focusing on my biological need for food, I open the fridge and realize I am almost out of bacon and eggs. How I wish I could just go back to the infinite options of the hotel breakfast buffet. There I didn't have to worry about groceries. There was enough food to satisfy my appetite, and best of all, I could grab one plate of food use it, then grab another and then another, with no care about having to clean the dirty dishes when I am done.  

I submerge myself in the dreadful repetition of routine and start cleaning my mess. One plate after anohter, auto pilot 

As I placed the last knife in the drying mat, next to the neatly stacked dishes, a sudden pang hit me. The satisfaction of having finished a task made me realize how ungrateful I have been. This laziness is a privilege. The frustration I felt because of my responsability of my household duties turns into guilt.

I notice that within the dirty clothes I spot the new jeans and t shirts I bought for my trip. I look back at the dishes that I cleaned and notice how my belly is full, the satisfaccion of a well cooked home meal. The more I observe I see the sings of a blessed life. 

Then this feeling takes me back to the people of Island. Even though the level of education is high in this country, poeple still believe in the stories crafted by vikings from the tales and myths that gave these group of poeple their distinctive status in history.
