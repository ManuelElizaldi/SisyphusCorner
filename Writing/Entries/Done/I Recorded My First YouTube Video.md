Saturday at my apartment, me and my girlfriend enjoy the nothingness together. She asks me to help her with her new hobby, a food blog where she records all of our culinary excursions. A simple and entertaining way to journal our dates and share it with the world. I don't mind being the investor in this project, for I get in return delicious food and the pleasure of her company. 

She knows I am a writer (or at least try to be) and asks for my advice for her blog. Our flows clash sometimes, whenever I suggest an addition to her punchy one liners, she says "Nah I don't want to write all that on here"  then I quizzically retort "Why are you not capitalizing proper nouns?" to which she explains "That's not my style." 

Two artists enjoying the creative tango of clashing styles. While she proceeds to do some final QA on the post, I remembered how I bought a high quality microphone with an Amazon Gift card I received, with the purpose of starting a YouTube Channel to share my thoughts on the books I have read.

The first contender was the Count of Monte Cristo. After finishing this brick, I wrote a Substack essay on the book and then made a small script for a video I intended to record. This never came and the idea went to the graveyard. Weeks later I started exploring Juan Rulfo's work in an effort to connect with my Mexican roots. I had hoped to be able to share what I found inside his spectral stories but alas, a loose sense of certainty stopped me before I even tried. Then I read 'The Stranger' by Camus and 'My Year of Rest and Relaxation' by Ottessa Moshfegh, two absurdist novels, each with its own approach to existence, but now I didn't have the confidence to articulate my ideas. Each new book I read was a possible video which never amounted to anything. The idea just remained a possibility. 

Following all these "failed attempts" I thought to myself, I just needed to study from the people that have already done it. So I did what any Youtubeaholic would do, and opened a 'How To Start a YouTube Channel' tutorial in hopes of being convinced to do something I knew I already wanted to do. And so the eternal carousel of preparation turned and turned. 

I admired how simple it was for my girlfriend to just put together a blog and send it on its way for the algorithm to do its thing, hoping to receive its blessing. Bestowing on her virality or some other hidden yearning of hers. While she uploaded the food blog, I confessed to her "I've always wanted to create a YouTube channel". 

After my confession, she just said "Just do it, don't be a pussy". She saved me with her direct honesty and remember clearly how my spirit ceased its existence for a micro second. A sudden cold behind the eye sockets of my skull. Was that really the answer? Of course it was. I have been doing this in another area of my life for many years.

This blatant statement of love reminded me of the mental muscle I have been training for some time now. I may not be a YouTuber (yet), but I am a runner. Even though I have ran enough kilometers to run down a horse, I still experience the inevitable friction before any workout.

That is just a reality, a natural human response. Repetition after repetition has taught me not to listen to my mind. In an absurd way, you just follow the body. My mind is lazy, complacent and in a dual contrarian existence, sometimes it punishes me for not doing enough. So, it is clear, this brain of mine is as trustworthy as a Schizophrenic Artic camel.  

Thinking "To hell with it", a sudden burning adrenaline started to build up inside of me. Feeling a bit insecure about my Mexican English accent I told myself I would just do a test and see how it goes. I still needed to check the lighting of my apartment and finally, after many weeks, try out the sound capturing capabilities of my microphone. 

An unexpected memory flooded my mind before I hit record. I recalled how this same insecurity had already lived inside of me, years before, when I first moved to Austin. Having grown up in Mexico, I never had a chance to submerge myself in English. Youtube, online video games and movies were my saviors. This media gave me confidence to learn the slang that would help me understand the people around me, but even though I knew the speech, I still had self doubt about my accent. Weeks after calling this central Texas town home I had plenty of interactions with my coworkers and people I met. Some loved my accent and what once was an imagined crutch, became my booster. 

I placed my phone on my tripod facing my bookshelf. Grabbed the winning candidate: The Pearl by John Steinbeck, since I had recently finished this book during my vacation and the ideas were ripe with juice. I hit record and just started rambling on. 

A test can't be wrong, so I watched it, not expecting anything from it. I was surprised by the result. Of course there were some evident gaps in my arguments, which were quickly fixed after I sat down at my desk, put pen to paper and drafted a quick script. My second try was even better. I enjoyed talking about how Steinbeck understood the Mexican economic system before I did. 

I saved the video files to my server and did some minimal editing. I uploaded the video to YouTube and wished it well.
