I arrived yesterday, from only an hour of sleep stacked on top of jet lag, there's still a hazy tiredness covering me. I intentionally asked for an additional PTO at my job to rest and in case a flight got delayed and ended up stuck somewhere in the transient journey back home. 

Fortunately, everything ran smoothly, even though we had 3 connecting flights, miraculously none got delayed. With the assurance that we made it back home safe, with all of our luggage I was able to lay down in my childhood bed and rest.

As I slowly started to yield to the dream world, I thought to myself with an air of gratitude what a privilege it is to sleep without an alarm. I woke up with the feeling of having stepped through a portal. Perhaps the new recent renovations done to my childhood home influenced this feeling, but there was a tangible sting of something new in the air. 

I made my way to the kitchen, instinctively knowing my mom had left half a pot of coffee. This knowledge has been ingrained in me after 20 something years of living in this house. I have a personal mug for when I am visiting my parents. A lucky find in Marshalls God knows how many years ago. It is a smokey blue ceramic mug, sporting a Ouija board, esoteric drawings of the moon and the sun and the words "Stay Wild Moon Child" engraved on it. 

Pouring the first cup of the day, I felt something that has been absent for a while. A sort of nothingness fueled by no engagement to an objective. No desire to extract value out of every second of the day. 

One of my revelations during my trip to Iceland - which I do plan on writing blog posts about here. Is that recently I have been living in high gear. There is a constant sense of time running out, which leads me to wanting to squeeze value out of every second. 

Every week day is a repetition of the previous one. I finish my work, go exercise and after a small break I turn to studying, writing for Substack or Linkedin, or work on other projects. I don't see the pursuit of objectives as a bad thing, the contrary is true for me, but a mindset shift is due. 

There's a passage in The Pearl by John Steinbeck after Kino finds a shell, this sort of describes what I need to start adopting. When Kino holds the shell and starts working to open it, hoping to find a pearl, Juana stares with attention but then turns away. Knowing that wanting something too much can drive the luck away. Perhaps in my stubbornness to live intensely I have frightened luck, turning possibility into fact.

So, with coffee in hand I stepped out to my parents' garden. I immediately noticed how green and grown it was compared to the last time I was here. My dad's care for plants and the constant rain in the region have done this little safe haven wonders. 

As I walked toward the chairs in the back side of the garden, I noticed that there was a spider web which I had to crouch under so as not to inflict cataclysmic damage to the spider's house, then sat down and enjoyed my coffee in my home, as I have done many times before. The nothingness of my day paired with the caffeine flowing was so comforting. 

After a couple minutes of contemplation, I decided to work a bit on an essay and questions for my book club with my girlfriend. This time we are discussing Martyr! by Kaveh Akbar. While writing, I remembered there's a dupe perfume shop nearby that I have been wanting to go to, so taking advantage of my visit to my home town, I made my way there. 

I am not sure why or how I came to enjoy walking so much. I could've easily driven there, it is only 5 minutes away from my house but instead opted for walking, which turns the trip into a 20 something minute endeavor but in return I get the opportunity for contemplation. 

There is a general pride some people feel when talking about their roots, the place where they became who they are. They boast about the growth and progress of their city. Some parade their local sports club colors like a peacock attracting a mate. I envy them, for I do appreciate my home town, but I have no boastful claim to make, I yield to the reality.    

This town was in the center of the Cartel War around 2010. Since then, the passage of time brings punishment instead of reward. Businesses once flourishing are now closed, windows are dirty and broken. The over growth shows the negligence for care. The streets are crater filled, making you wonder if you are on the moon or in a border town in Mexico. People I once knew don't live here anymore, the roots are here but the trunk has been moved elsewhere seeking better sunshine. 

I walked through the city center, it is not a hot day, there is a cold breeze flowing. I walked with intention, but still people stare at me. I feel like an outsider. Perhaps living away from the Rio Bravo has imprinted a foreigner aura to me. Noticeable to anybody that walks near me. 

I finally made it to the store, bought a dupe of Tobacco Vanille by Tom Ford. I sprayed some on me, even though I am wearing my workout clothes and enjoy the fact that I paid three hundred pesos instead of the $300 USD. The witchy inventiveness of the Mexican never cease to amaze me. 

With renovated pride and smelling like a million dollars I made my way back home. I consider myself a student of Carl Jung, seeking omens of validation in the universe for my hard work. But today, there was no hunt. Only the nothingness.   

That is when a stranger approached me. Bald headed, wearing a white button down shirt, stained with some sweat, shave due because I noticed some facial hair starting to grow, blue jeans and cowboy boots. He asked me "isn't this a beautiful country?" this caught off guard, I only responded "The most beautiful." For some reason he started talking to me in English, again that foreigner aura denouncing me.

He started telling me about how he used to live in San Antonio, Texas, but he went through hardships dealt by the another type of cartel. A systematic oppression by the police and the justice system. He didn't go into too much detail, although I wanted him to, maybe I should've asked what happened, but he only mentioned that his kids were kidnapped. By who? How? or why? are unknown to me.

Then he told me about how he moved to Mexico after this. Started a company and now is making millions. He showed a sort of optimistic pride one has after succeeding with a well crafted plan. 

After that he proceeded to tell me about how the money now a days is in technical skills, carpentry, plumbing, jobs done with the hands. He mentioned how computers are sort of good but insisted on me learning how to build with my hands. Was this some sort of omen? Synchronicity embodied in this strange joyful individual? How did he know I worked with computers? 

He said "Good luck to you!" then I responded "God bless, have a good one!" and waved him good bye as he rode out into town in the blue pick up Chevy he recently bought and showed me with great enthusiasm. I tried to remember when was the last time I said "God bless" to someone. Without a clear answer I decided to keep walking to my home.

On my way back I saw the Doctor Simi mascot wearing the Mexico national team jersey dancing in front of the pharmacy, said good morning to him and kept going. I kept thinking about the man and that strange interaction.
