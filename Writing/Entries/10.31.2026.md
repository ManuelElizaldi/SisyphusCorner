It is Halloween, the weather has been cooling down a bit, and today I didn't want to do the work. I've been working on writing my review of the book Stoner by John Williams. I had promised myself I'd finish this piece yesterday, but I didn't. 

I woke up early, chugged the water I had left in my bed side table and marched onward to start my Saturday. I turned on ol'Bertha, my espresso machine, and gave her a couple of seconds to heat up. Every time I start her up you can hear something going on inside of it rattling and making all sorts of noises. 

Right now I am using a blend of coffee I bought in a recent trip I made to Mexico City. The beans were roasted by Kiyo in Roma Norte. This blend comes from Oaxaca. Betraying my well honed habit of drinking medium roasts, lately I have been gravitating towards lighter roasts. Some so-and-so coffee expert once told me that lighter roasts allow the taste of the beans to show up more in the cup. I don't think that is why I bought this light roast, I just wanted to switch things up. I have to keep myself in my toes.  

Continuing with the ritual, I ground up the coffee a bit coarser than usual. I have been getting better extractions this way. I prepared my espresso puck by shifting around the grounds, a bit of shaking and a bit of thumping, and then I was blessed by the coffee gods with some caramel crema floating on top of my liquid caffeine.

I've been trying to learn how to make latte art. One would assume it would come natural to me since I've been partaking in the habit of caffeination for almost 13 years, but execution does not come with appreciation. 

I steamed the milk just like the thousands of 'how-to videos' I have watched YouTube. The milk looked silky. I thought to myself "Nice, this time I will be able to draw a tulip." Then like a clinical surgeon I tilted the espresso cup with my left hand and with the right I poured in circular motions laying the foundation of my canvas. 

From there I poured white paint strokes. I saw how the milk laid on top of the crema making white streaks I saw some white streaks and how the milk was laying on the top of the coffee. I get excited because my progress is promising, but somehow my excitement makes me pour too hard, and I ended up with a wiggly line. Well, I guess this is abstract art. 

To celebrate this spooky day, I sat down on my brown leathered sofa, put on a hoodie and cranked the AC. I could feel the crisp chilly air in my apartment cuddle me while I held my hot coffee cup.

I started an episode of 'Widow's Bay' and while watching this comedy, horror masterpiece of a show I remembered that the creator, Katie Dippold took 20 years to develop her idea what we have now. Maybe I too, will take 20 years to master my latte art.
