It is Halloween, the weather has been cooling down a bit, and today I didn't want to do the work. I've been working on writing my review of the book Stoner by John Williams. I had promised myself I'd finish this piece yesterday, but I didn't. 

I woke up early, chugged the water I had left in my bed side table and marched onward to start my Saturday. I turned on ol'Bertha, my espresso machine, and gave her a couple of seconds to heat up. Every time I start turn her on I can hear something going on inside of it rattling and making all sorts of noises. 

Right now I am using a blend of coffee I bought in a recent trip I made to Mexico City. The beans were roasted by Kiyo in Roma Norte. This blend comes from Oaxaca. Betraying my well honed habit of drinking medium roasts. lately I have been gravitating towards lighter roasts. Some so-and-so coffee expert once told me that lighter roasts allow the taste of the beans to show up more in the cup. I don't think that is why I bought this light roast, I just wanted to switch things up. I have to keep myself on my toes.  

Continuing with the ritual, I ground up the coffee a bit coarser than usual. I have been getting better extractions this way. I prepared my espresso puck by shifting around the grounds, a bit of shaking and a bit of thumping, and then I was blessed by the coffee gods with some caramel crema floating on top of my liquid caffeine.

I've been trying to learn how to make latte art for some time now. One would assume it would come natural to me since I've been partaking in the habit of caffeination for almost 13 years, but execution does not come with appreciation. 

I steamed the milk just like the thousands of 'how-to videos' I've watched on YouTube. The milk looked silky. I thought to myself "Nice, this time I will be able to draw a tulip." Then like a clinical surgeon I tilted the espresso cup with my left hand and with the right I poured in circular motions laying the foundation of my canvas. 

From there, I poured white paint strokes. I saw how the milk laid on top of the espresso crema making white streaks. I got excited because my progress seemed promising, but somehow my excitement made me pour too hard, and I ended up with a wiggly line. Well, content with my attempt I concluded this was an abstract tulip.

To celebrate this spooky day, I cranked the AC, grabbed a hoodie and planted myself in my brown leather sofa. I could feel the crisp chilly air in my apartment cuddle me while I held my hot coffee cup.

I started an episode of 'Widow's Bay' and while watching this comedy, horror masterpiece of a show I remembered that the creator, Katie Dippold took 20 years to develop her idea to what we have now. Maybe I too, will take 20 years to master my latte art. 

After being scared enough to kick-start my adrenaline I jumped out of my comfort dwellings and headed out to workout. I got in my car and drove up the top floor of my parking lot. Up there I have a great view of the Austin city skyline. Also, I provide some amusing entertainment to the bird flying around watching me hurdle around a pair of kettlebells and 100 lb sandbag. 

The weight was not too heavy today. While throwing the weight over my head, cleaning it and carrying it around I noticed some wet spots in the ground where it had rain yesterday. I looked up at the gloomy gray sky and wondered if Austin was going to be blessed with more water. "Hopefully I love the rain" I thought. I only did a quick 30 minute circuit session.  

Back in my apartment, I remembered the Stoner piece I promised myself I would write. Just like the character in this novel I tried organizing my bookshelf, hoping to organize the ideas inside my head, but today I didn't want to do the work, so I left the bookshelf half organized. I left this task for my future self.

I could smell and feel the remnants of my physical effort from my workout. So I headed to the shower, but Before jumping in I observed with a greater degree of acceptance the new spot in my head where hair started to thin out. 

I first noticed this last night after arriving home from a date with my girlfriend, while I was getting ready for bed. I was cleaning my face and when I finished I looked up at the mirror and the new spot was there, in the crown of my head.

I moved my hair to get a closer look, but it was kind of hard to see since it was in the top of my head. I thought about how I'm a couple of weeks away from my 30th birthday. My girlfriend was to the left of me and I said to her pointing at the spot "look, my hair is thinning from here now."

At that moment, I didn't know what to feel or what to think. Just like I can't yet find the words for my article describing what the novel Stoner did to me. 

I knew I had to embrace acceptance, after all I've been a first-hand witness to what time does. I had to recently renew my passport and comparing both old and


when I looked at my new document I could clearly see my receding hairline. 

Starting to accept this new change I thought about how I've always loved the bald and ripped look. Similar to the anime character Saitama from One Punch Man.

My girlfriend trying to comfort me said "You are so handsome and I love you." Embracing her warm love I hugged her and said "Me too amors."

I don't have much to say, after all today I didn't want to do the work.
