October came and went like the falling leaves in the tree. The crisp air blowing around starts to smell different, and the weather starts to cool down. With this chill houses start to dress up in different festive costumes. You start to see monsters crawling up from the lawn, then they are replaced by pumpkins and turkeys to finally end with a show of Christmas lights. 

For some reason, October has always felt like the beginning of a new beginning. My birthday starts to loom closer (Sagittarius gang represent!) and the year starts to march to an end. This then makes me think about everything I've done and haven't throughout the year.

October has also marked a constant in my life since 2007. Back then one of my greatest pleasures was to watch [The Angry Video Game Nerd](https://www.youtube.com/@JamesNintendoNerd). His main jazz was doing reviews of very bad old video games. But from time to time, he would entertain his audience with reviews of cult classic horror films. 

During that year of our Lord, he started a series called [Monster Madness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSvjAJvempE&t=39s), where he did one review a day during the month of October. This inspired me to watch a horror movie a day during this month. 

Each year October came, and new spooky surprises were dealt with each passing day. Keeping the movie a day pace has become harder the older I get but still, the spook fest is well and alive.  

This month was a blessing in this regard, and since I am now sharing my life experiences with the people of the interwebs, I wanted to share some of the best things I have enjoyed during this past month.

Spooky month is over, but that doesn't mean the spook stops. 

# Movies
![[Pasted image 20261105180450.png]]

Starting off strong with Lee Cronin's The Mummy. I thought this movie was going to be a cheap refried version of the action packed classics from the 2000s - The Mummy Returns & The Scorpion King. I watched this movie in the worst possible place one could watch a scary movie: A plane. What can I say? I like to keep myself on my toes. 

I wanted to scream, but being a courteous person I restrained myself and just absorbed each blow. 

This movie mixes the anguish a family goes through from having their daughter kidnapped and the helpless dread from watching her be consumed by an ancient Egyptian curse. Also including a lovely sprinkle of body horror. The scene where they cut the daughter's nails still makes my body twitch. This is what The Exorcist would've been like if the possessing demon was born in Egypt.

Doing a bit of research for this article I found out that there's also a couple of 1940s 'The Mummy' movies I plan to watch some other time! 

![[Pasted image 20261105181431.png]]

Sinister, starting my man, Ethan Hawke. I decided to rewatch this movie because a friend of mine sent me an Instagram post saying that this movie, according to scientists, was the scariest movie of all time. I thought to myself, "What a bold claim." So I had to test out this hypothesis. 

I remember watching this movie when it was first released, and I was scared but not as these so-called scientists claimed. Also, what type of scientist do you have to be in order to study the scariness level of a movie? Anyway, the movie follows Ellison, a true-crime writer chasing his former glory, but feeling frustrated about not being able to reach it, since he's plagued by writer's block. 

He moves his family to the house where the crime of his new book was committed - What a way to fight writer's block. While moving in to their new home, Ellison discovers a box of old Super 8 reels, which he thinks is the key to unlocking his writing. There's a scene where he is surrounded by darkness, the only color you can see come from his whisky and his tranced eyes staring at the projector. From there on out, he starts to spiral into madness and horror unfolds. 

This movie skips the happy ending, which is how I like my horror, but I still don’t agree with the scientists. Regardless, I recommend it.

What's with all these writers getting into messed up situations? Should I be worried? 

Hokum starts Adam Scott, which I first met in Parks and Rec, then saw in Severance. And now he is starring in the big screen on Hokum. This follows the life of another writer as he is dealing with the death of his parents. He embarks on a trip to a resort in Ireland were his parents celebrated their honeymoon, with the intent of scattering their ashes in the nearby forest. 

Soon after arriving, he discovers there's a Cailleach - an ancient witch from Gaelic and Irish folklore - holding these lands on its claws and using the main character's guilt against him.

This film felt new because it mixes the stories of old and how their unnatural, unknown energies are used to torment the character from within. 

The cinematography in this is amazing too, the setting helps a lot, and it is put to good use. The Irish forest sometimes looks welcoming, and other times like a trap set by the Cailleach. The scenery around the resort made me crave a Guinness beer in Ireland, although I don't want to stumble around any folk creatures.   

Hopefully more directors employ more folklore into their horror movies.

Clayface
Meet the bastard child of 'The Substance' and 'Obsession'. As soon as the movie ended, I had to scream and shake my body. There was so much tension within my muscles, bones and nerves. I am glad this movie doesn't have a 4D version.

This was a very well executed experiment by DC Studios. It is such twisted concept, and I feel like the Superhero/Anti Hero genre needs a refresher from the amusement park like films we had throughout recent years. 

Here you follow the life of a Movie Star. He is about to break it big, then filled with hubris, he faces (pun intended) the consequences of his actions. A group of high bravado Akrham Mafiosos butcher his moneymaker (face). He looses everything and in desperation he decides to undergo an experimental procedure to get his life back, which of course, results in a horrifying mess. 

During the experiment scenes my body wanted to close my eyes, but I resisted. For some masochistic reason I held them open, absorbing every bit of pain of anti hero was going through.

I haven't seen many body horror movies, but this has to be one of the best, if not the best.

Resident Evil
Finally, this movie also inspired the next section of this blog post. From a horror standpoint, I believe it out did the previous 2000s Resident Evil movies, again, same as with The Mummy, this new version leans harder into the horror. 

I haven't played the games too much, so I didn't catch many of the references and fan services scattered around this film. So a rewatch is scheduled for some time in the near future.

This movie starts with a bang (literally), it drops you right in the middle of a snow storm. Our hero is oblivious to what is happening and that brings a humorous suspense. 

The zombies have a real texture to them, which lead me to research how this movie was made and apparently minimal CGI was used. Those creatures are disgustingly beautiful. 

There are rumors of a 2nd movie, which I am excited for. 
 
# Video Games
![[Pasted image 20261106144821.png]]
Inspired by the movie, I looked up the Resident Evil games, to my surprise Capcom was having a Halloween sale and got my copy of Resident Evil 2 for only $8 USD. 

I haven't played too much horror games, I usually stick to RPGs so learning the ropes was something I truly enjoyed. This game was a great remake from the original 1998. They added new story lines and the revamped graphics scared me more than once. 

I am currently in my second play through and killing zombies and solving puzzles while malevolent forces chase you down never stops raising my hearth rate.
