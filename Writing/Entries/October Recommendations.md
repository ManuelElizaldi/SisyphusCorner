October came and went like the falling leaves in the tree. The crisp air blowing around starts to smell different, and the weather starts to cool down. With this chill houses start to dress up in different festive costumes. You start to see monsters crawling up from the lawn, then they are replaced by pumpkins and turkeys to finally end with a show of Christmas lights. 

For some reason, October has always felt like the beginning of a new beginning. My birthday starts to loom closer (Sagittarius gang represent!) and the year starts to march to an end. This then makes me think about everything I've done and haven't throughout the year.

October has also marked a constant in my life since 2007. Back then one of my greatest pleasures was to watch [The Angry Video Game Nerd](https://www.youtube.com/@JamesNintendoNerd). His main jazz was doing reviews of very bad old video games. But from time to time, he would entertain his audience with reviews of cult classic horror films. 

During that year of our Lord, he started a series called [Monster Madness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSvjAJvempE&t=39s), where he did one review a day during the month of October. This inspired me to watch a horror movie a day during this month. 

Each year October came, and new spooky surprises were dealt with each passing day. Keeping the movie a day pace has become harder the older I get but still, the spook fest is well and alive.  

This month was a blessing in this regard, and since I am now sharing my life experiences with the people of the interwebs, I wanted to share some of the best things I have enjoyed during this past month.

Spooky month is over, but that doesn't mean the spook stops. 

Starting of with **Movies**:

Starting off strong with Lee Cronin's The Mummy. I thought this movie was going to be a cheap refried version of the action packed classics from the 2000s - The Mummy Returns & The Scorpion King. I watched this movie in the worst possible place one could watch a scary movie: A plane. What can I say? I like to keep myself on my toes. 

This movie mixes the anguish a family goes through from having their daughter kidnapped and the helpless dread from having her consumed by an ancient Egyptian curse. Of course, with a lovely sprinkle of body horror. 

This is what The Exorcists would've been like if the possessing demon was born in Egypt. It manages suspense really well and there are some scenes in there that made my skin tingle with anxiety. I have to give props to the SFX team on this one as well.

Doing a bit of research for this article I found out that there's also a couple of 1940s 'The Mummy' movies I plan to watch some other time! 

![[Pasted image 20261105181431.png]]

Sinister, starting my man, Ethan Hawke. I decided to rewatch this movie because a friend of mine sent me an Instagram post saying that this movie, according to scientists, was the scariest movie of all time. I thought to myself, "What a bold claim." So I had to test out this hypothesis. 

I remember watching this movie when it was first released, and I was scared but not as these so-called scientists claimed. Also, what type of scientist do you have to be in order to study the scariness level of a movie? Anyway, the movie follows Ellison, a true-crime writer chasing his former glory, but feeling frustrated about not being able to reach it, since he's plagued by writer's block. 

He moves his family to the house where the crime of his new book was committed - What a way to fight writer's block - then he starts to spiral into madness and horror unfolds. 

I don't agree with the scientists, but still, this is a great spooky watch.

![[Pasted image 20261106125853.png]]

What's with all these writers getting into messed up situations? Should I be worried? 

Hokum starts Adam Scott, which I first met in Parks and Rec, then in Severance. And now he is starting in the big screen on Hokum. This follows the life of another writer as he is dealing with the death of his parents. He embarks on a trip to a resort in Ireland were his parents celebrated their honeymoon, with the intent of scattering their ashes in the nearby forest. 

Soon after arriving he discovers there is a curse holding these lands on its claws. I can't recall if I had ever seen a movie that uses Irish folk tales as a vehicle to deliver a smack of horror, but this was a great execution. I am going to give it extra praise because the horror felt new and vastly different from what I had seen recently.

Finally, this movie also inspired the following section of this blog post. From a horror standpoint, I believe it out did the previous 2000s versions. Of course, I am talking about Resident Evil. 

I haven't played the games too much, so I didn't catch many of the references and fan service scattered around this film. So a rewatch is in order for some time in the future. 

This has some sick special effects, which from what I have seen, minimal CGI was used. It blends humor, suspense and disgustingly beautiful zombies. 

There are rumors of a 2nd movie, which I am excited for.
