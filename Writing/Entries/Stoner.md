[[Literature]] [[Book Review]]
It was getting late and sleep was slowly tightening its grip on me. It may be that I am becoming old or simply that I'm becoming a creature of habit. I have this ritual where I have to read for a couple of minutes before going to bed. Doing so signals my brain to shut off operations for the day, during this time my only task is to relax and surrender to Morpheus. 

Before I had this ritual in place, I watched YouTube, frying my dopamine receptors until I was anesthetized.

My time with Stoner by John Williams was coming to a close. I had 2 chapters left. My plan was to read one that night, and leave the last for tomorrow morning. I finished the second to last chapter, placed the coffee bag label I was using as a bookmark inside and closed the book. 

The brain omitted the signal, it was hard to succumb to sleep. I thought about how we never notice when we fall asleep. It just happens reducing hours to a mere instant to conclude with the beginning of a new day. A second though followed this, "funny how almost every living thing sleeps". Then a third idea, I thought about William Stoner, his presence kept nagging at me. Why did I felt so melancholic about him? Staring at the roof of my room I didn't have the answer, but I knew I had to continue reading. 

I finished the book that night, I knew how it would end. The first chapter of the book tells you how the life of Stoner ends. But I felt a tightness in my chest, similar to how it feels like to say goodbye to a lifelong friend you haven't seen in a while, not knowing when I will see him again.

This was clearly result of John William's simple, yet evocative prose. Following Stoner's life, from a humble farmer to a literature doctorate creates an emotional attachment to the character. Through this shock of emotions a mirror emerged which allowed me to see my life through the lens of William Stoner. 

I came to several conclusions which I plan to share here.

# I envy Stoner, for his passion found him.

Stoner did not find his passion, it found him. I looked back at my life, and all I saw what seemed like an eternal search. Reading Stoner's life I saw a man that lived for literature, everything he did was aimed at the well-being of his students and the furthering of his studies. It is never mentioned in the book, but I doubt that he ever thought about being anything else but a teacher.

Stoner entered college to study Agriculture with the aim to help his father with the family farm. Due to the curriculum, he had to take a perfunctory class in English.

William struggled during the first weeks of the class, not getting satisfactory grades in his exams. He read and reread the books assigned to him, but not going farther than thinking words are just words on paper, finding no use for them. 

During one lecture, Sloane, his teacher then and mentor after, was reading a passage by Shakespeare and proceeds to ask his class what it meant.

```
“What does the sonnet mean?” he asked abruptly, and paused, his eyes
searching the room with a grim and almost pleased hopelessness.

Sloane’s eyes came back to William Stoner, and he said dryly, “Mr.
Shakespeare speaks to you across three hundred years, Mr. Stoner; do you
hear him?”
```

When Sloane asked Stoner directly, something inside of him was shaken, he became aware of his existence, he felt his cloths touching his skin, his breath coming and going from his body, he noticed how the light shone on and bounced off his classmates. Besides this awareness he struggled to provide an answer but from that day on, literature started to nourish his life. 

```
He became conscious of himself in a way that he had not done before.
Sometimes he looked at himself in a mirror, at the long face with its thatch
of dry brown hair, and touched his sharp cheekbones; he saw the thin wrists
that protruded inches out of his coat sleeves; and he wondered if he
appeared as ludicrous to others as he did to himself.
```

A couple of years later after graduating from his English Literature undergraduate program, World War I breaks out. Stoner's friends were ready to answer the call to arms. But William was not sure about enlisting. He pays Sloane a visit - his mentor - to make up his mind. Perhaps, due to his years of experience or maybe seeing some part of him in Stoner, Sloane observed without hesitation:

```
“It’s love, Mr. Stoner,” Sloane said cheerfully. “You are in love. It’s as
simple as that.”
```

One decision had set flight to Stoner's life.

When I first join my current company, many reports ran manually, taking valuable hours from my coworkers day and exposing the process to potential errors. Taking advantage of the skills I had acquired, I got surgical and automated everything I could. My teammates stopped wasting hours of their time in tedious tasks and could focus on more valuable projects. This then overtime led to more patients being scheduled at our clinics. I had seen the impact and felt a level of satisfaction, but was I in love? 

As the automated morning reports run, my only job is to monitor, so time is offered to me, I take advantage of this gift and use it to work on personal projects. I sometimes grab my current read and immerse myself in the pages, other times I continue writing these essays but in the space in between these moments I flirt with the idea "What if?"

Looking out the window I daydream about a different life, unleashing a foray of alternate stories. These might not even be my ideas, but persuasions instilled in me by social media or the thousands of movies I devour. These ideas make me imagine myself as a writer, or sometimes as a painter, living by the arts. There was a time when I played the drums, but now that is forgotten. I'm well aware of my priorities, and my job is what gives me sustenance, but still I flirt with the idea of doing something else. 

The idea of love for my work is not foreign to me. Back in 2023, I was laid off from the company I was working with. The hours once invested in increasing shareholder value now were aimed at working on myself.

I woke up early, went for a run then came home, applied to jobs and worked on a personal programming projects for my portfolio. 

One day running the all mighty source of creativity illuminated me. Since money was short and my desire to become a good runner high, I decided to program my own digital coach. For this project I had to take every decision. Each step unlocked new skills, each new skill inspired new features for my project.

Each day seemed a copy the previous one, as time moved on I ingrained myself more in the crevasse of routine, and inevitably I fell in love with my craft.

One weekend, during that time my parents came to visit to check on how I was doing. We were having beers after dinner and I clearly remember the worried faces of my parents. They tried to act calm and collected, my dad filled each pocket of silence with by bombarding me with questions "you like where you live? Did you enjoy your dinner? Are you eating well? Do you need anything from us? Why don't you move back to Laredo?" 

Sharing the blood that flows through us, makes us emotionally connected. *something here* 

There was a tension in my chest, tightening further with each trivial question. I furrowed my brow from the heavy air around the dining table. My body was tingling by sensing their worry, so trying to tame the tense atmosphere I shared a conclusion I reached when building my digital coach project "These past months, I found out that I would still go into this field of work even if I had known that I was going to be laid off."

To this, my father asked "So, you truly love what you do?" 

"I love it, it has blessed me with so much," said I. 

The days passed and with them a new beginning. I felt a tug to write about my experience building this project and share it with the world. This added another dimension of joy to the process where I documented each step. I posted several coding blogs and one lead me to landing my current job.

Now, I must recognize that my love for my career has dimmed for a moment, but that was my own doing. I asked too much of my work. I rushed through each project, extracting the knowledge I thought would best serve me for a new opportunity. Everything needed a reason of being, not letting myself be and enjoy the present moment. 



Perhaps I was not in Sloane's lecture hall when he asked what Shakespeare's sonnets meant, but after reading about Stoner's life the love that has dimmed for my career has slowly started to glow anew. 

I have realized that I was asking too much of my work. I was using it as a means to an end.




Although the love has dimmed at this moment in my career, reading Stoner has provided me solace.

I have to begin at the prose of this book. I was mesmerized by how simple but elegant John William's writing is. I fear that if I ever tried to replicate his style I would probably just faint in revulsion at the atrocious words I would write.
