It was getting late and sleep was slowly tightening its grip on me. It may be that I am becoming old or simply that I'm becoming a creature of habit. I have this ritual where I have to read for a couple of minutes before going to bed. This signals my brain to shut off operations for the day, the only task is to relax and surrender to Morpheus. 

Before I had this ritual in place, I watched YouTube, frying my dopamine receptors until I was anesthetized.

My time with Stoner by John Williams was coming to a close. I had 2 chapters left. My plan was to read one that night, and leave the last for tomorrow morning. I finished the second to last chapter, placed the coffee bag label I was using as a bookmark inside and closed the book. 

The brain omitted the signal, it was hard to succumb to sleep. I thought about how we never notice when we fall asleep. It just happens reducing hours to a mere instant to conclude with the beginning of a new day. A second though followed this, "funny how almost every living thing sleeps". Then a third idea, I thought about William Stoner, his presence kept nagging at me. Why did I felt so melancholic about him? I didn't have the answer, but I knew I had to continue reading. 

I finished the book that night, I knew how it would end. The beginning of the book tells you how the life of Stoner ends. But still it felt like I was saying goodbye to a lifelong friend, not knowing when it would be the next time I was going to see him.

This was clearly result of John William's simple, yet evocative prose. Following Stoner's life, from a humble farmer to a literature doctorate creates an emotional attachment to the character. Through this shock of emotions a mirror emerged which allowed me to see my life through the lens of William Stoner. 

I came to several conclusions which I plan to share here.

# I envy Stoner, for his passion found him.

Stoner did not find his passion, it found him. I looked back at my life, and all I saw what seemed like an eternal search. Reading Stoner's life I saw a man that lived for literature, everything he did was aimed at the wellbeing of his students and the furthering of his studies. It is never mentioned in the book, but I doubt that he ever thought about being anything else but a teacher.

Stoner entered college to study Agriculture with the aim to help his father with the family farm. Due to the curriculum, he had to take a perfunctory class in English.

One decision had set flight to Stoner's life.

When I first join my current company, many reports ran manually, taking valuable hours from my coworkers day and exposing the process to potential errors. Taking advantage of the skills I had acquired, I got surgical and automated everything I could. My teammates stopped wasting hours of their time in tedious tasks and could focus on more valuable projects. This then overtime led to more patients being scheduled at our clinics. I had seen the impact and felt a level of satisfaction, but was I in love? 

As the automated morning reports run, my only job is to monitor, so time is offered to me, I take advantage of this gift and use it to work on personal projects. I sometimes grab my current read and immerse myself in the pages, other times I continue writing these essays but in the space between these moments I flirt with the idea "What if?"

Looking out the window I Daydream about a different life unleashing a foray of alternate stories. These might not even be my ideas, but persuasions instilled in me by social media or the thousands of movies I devour. These ideas make me imagine myself as a writer, or sometimes as a painter, living by the arts. There was a time when I played the drums, but now that is forgotten. I'm well aware of my priorities, and my job is what gives me sustenance, but still I flirt with the idea of doing something else. 

The idea of love for my work is not foreign to me. Back in 2023, I was laid off from the company I was working with. The hours once invested in increasing shareholder value now were aimed at working on myself.

I woke up early, went for a run then came home, applied to jobs and worked on a personal programming projects. 

One day running the all mighty source of creativity illuminated me. Since money was short and my desire to become a good runner high, I decided to program my own digital coach. For this project I had to take every decision. Each step unlocked new skills, each new skill inspired new features for my project.

Each day seemed a copy the previous one, as time moved on I ingrained myself more in the crevasse of routine, and inevitably I fell in love with my craft.

One time when my parents came to visit that year, we were having beers after dinner. I clearly remember the worried faces of my parents. They tried to act calm and collected, but sharing the blood that flows through us, also makes us share an emotional connection between each other. There is an unsaid understanding about how we are feeling. 

I felt the tension in my chest, furrowed my brow from the heavy air around the dining table. My body was tingling by sensing their worry, so trying to tame the tense atmosphere I shared a conclusion I reached when building my project "These past months, I found out that I would still go into this field of work even if I had known that I was going to be laid off."

To this, my father asked "So, you truly love what you do?" 

"I love it, it has blessed me with so much," said I. 

The days passed, with little to no change. I felt the need to write about my approach to building this project and share it with the world. Incredibly, this added another dimension of joy to the process. Writing about what I was building unlocked another dimension of joy, which then helped me land my current role.

# Notes
My objective with this piece is to write a bit about Stoner, some initial thoughts and attempt at answering the question "What did this book do to me?"

I have to begin at the prose of this book. I was mesmerized by how simple but elegant John William's writing is. I fear that if I ever tried to replicate his style I would probably just faint in revulsion at the atrocious words I would write. His style is elegant without being pretentious, I can clearly notice the years of experience in how sew his ideas into words. 

One example of this prose is when he describes what love is for our character Stoner. We follow Stoner throughout his attempt at love, like icraus flying too close to the sun / but never quite reaching it in his own household  he discovered love and then experiences the disappointment of not having his love is not mutual. Then Williams lays down one of the best descriptions of what is love. 

Love for knowledge - I, like Stoner, truly enjoy the pleasure of reading, learning new things.
